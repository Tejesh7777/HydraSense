Euclidean Distance (L2 Geometric Metric)
d_E(u, v) = √( Σ (u_i - v_i)² ) = ‖u - v‖₂

Represents straight-line geometric distance in standardized multidimensional space. Highly sensitive to extreme coordinate outliers because differences are squared. Ideal for spherical cluster topologies.

Manhattan Distance (L1 Cityblock Metric)
d_M(u, v) = Σ |u_i - v_i| = ‖u - v‖₁

Measures grid-based taxicab distance along each independent physicochemical axis. Robust against disproportionate spikes in single variables (e.g., grease or turbidity shock loads) without skewing the overall norm.

Algorithmic Comparison Findings (SIH26023 Objective 2)
1. Euclidean Distance (L2 Norm) Characteristics:

Euclidean distance excels at recognizing dense, hyperspherical clusters in normalized parameter space. However, because deviations are squared, a sudden transient spike in kitchen COD (e.g. from 200 to 800 mg/L) causes an exponential increase in distance, sometimes causing samples with identical pH, TDS, and turbidity to be grouped separately.

2. Manhattan Distance (L1 Norm) Characteristics:

Manhattan distance treats each physicochemical parameter linearly along its respective coordinate axis. This prevents single-parameter surges (like detergent surfactants or food solids) from overwhelming the distance metric, leading to higher cluster separation stability and superior alignment in mixed domestic greywater streams.
